Border artefacts · a field guide

How to spot, build, and steward a border artefact

A working guide for the RF team. A border artefact is a position, not a thing. It is defined by who sits at the border, what it coordinates, and how long it lives. Its form (data, a canvas, a strategy, a contract, a convening) is incidental. This guide gives the mental models, the test, the roles, and three worked shapes, so you can derive the right artefact for any theme.

1 · What it is

A border artefact is not an object. It is a relationship: a shared thing that sits on the edge between a few named actors and lets them coordinate without merging or agreeing on everything.

It is the working cousin of the "boundary object": plastic enough to fit each actor's local use, robust enough to hold one identity across all of them. What makes it a border artefact is where it sits, not what it is made of.

The artefact sits in no one's turf. It sits in the border between named actors. RF stewards it; RF never owns it.

That neutrality is load-bearing, not an ethic. Its power comes from being on nobody's turf, which is exactly what lets actors adopt it without ceding control. The moment one actor owns it, it stops being a commons and becomes a platform-grab, and the others defect. RF can hold the border precisely because it is not competing for anyone's turf.

Its utility is three questions. If you cannot answer all three, you do not yet have an artefact.

Who is at the border
A few named actors, each with a distinct unmet incentive. Never "everybody".
What is its purpose
The specific misalignment it resolves, and what crosses the border in each direction.
How long it lives
Transient (hands off to a successor) or enduring (becomes infrastructure).
Write any artefact in one line:
[artefact] sits between [named actors] so that [what crosses / purpose]; it is a [form], [transient → successor / enduring]. RF stewards it because [why it is on no one's turf]. OAQ: the open air-quality commons sits between communities and solution actors so that all of them act on the same air numbers; a commons, enduring. RF stewards it because it competes with none of them. Waste (dry): the waste canvas sits between innovators, funders and the housing ministry so all three see the same broken material-recovery chokepoint; a diagnostic, transient → the professionalised-MRF feedstock-market policy. RF stewards it because it holds no waste asset and clears no tender. Waste (wet): the artefact has not crystallised. It will sit between air-quality, water and waste actors so that landfill methane and leachate become one measured problem. Form and life are not yet set; the intersection is the nursery.

2 · Seven mental models

The whole method falls out of the three questions.

1

The named edge, not the public square

A border has sides, and you must be able to name who is on each. "Open data for everyone" is not a border artefact; it is a public good no one champions. The power comes from specific actors with specific unmet incentives facing each other across the object. If the border is "everybody", reject it: there is no edge to hold.

2

Form follows the border

The artefact takes whatever shape that edge needs. Data is only one option: a diagnostic (a canvas or map that makes the broken link visible), a commons (an open registry or data substrate), a strategy (a PPP standard, a benefit-sharing rule), a contract (a model clause that pulls actors in), or a convening (the coalition itself as the object). Sometimes the artefact is the strategy. The border, purpose and life are essential; the material is incidental.

3

The form-by-life grid, and artefacts move across it

Two axes place every artefact, and its job is often to travel from one cell to another. A diagnostic canvas is strategy-and-transient; its purpose is to migrate to strategy-and-enduring (the policy it points to).

TransientEnduring
DataA one-off study (quantify landfill methane once to make the case)The OAQ commons, an open registry
StrategyThe waste canvas (diagnose, then retire)The professionalised-MRF policy, an ABS mechanism, a standard
4

Succession: know when to let one die

An artefact is born for a purpose. When it is served, it either becomes permanent infrastructure or hands off to a successor and retires. The canvas identifies the broken MRF, spawns the policy artefact, and its job is done. The failure mode is the zombie artefact: a canvas kept alive as a deliverable because no one named its successor. RF's discipline is running the lifecycle, not preserving any single object.

5

The intersection is a nursery

Where two theme-borders overlap, a new artefact crystallises with a new cast of actors. Wet waste is unsolved precisely because its artefact (air-quality, water and waste actors, around methane and leachate) has not been born yet. That is not a gap, it is the site. And the strategic kicker: only an actor working across waste, air and water can even see that intersectional artefact. A deep single-theme funder is blind to it. Intersectional artefacts are what a wide portfolio can uniquely steward.

6

Neutrality is load-bearing

Because the artefact sits between named actors and RF is on none of their turf, RF can hold the edge. The instant RF, or any one actor, owns it, the border collapses into a platform-grab and the others defect. Steward, never own: not as ethics, as physics.

7

It is all chlorophyll

Data-artefact or strategy-artefact, diagnostic or standard, every one lives in the same layer of the tree: the conversion of RF's four capitals into ownership on the ground. The verticals are where; the artefacts are how the light becomes growth. That is what unifies them.

3 · The test

Six questions. The old checklist described a commons; this screens any form, including a strategy or a diagnostic.

  • Name the sidesWho is at the border? If the honest answer is "everybody", it is a public good, not an artefact.
  • What crossesName what flows each way (data one way, solutions back; feedstock one way, rules the other).
  • The misalignmentWhat specific incentive gap or broken link does it resolve between those actors?
  • The form, for nowDiagnostic, commons, strategy, contract or convening? Pick the one this edge needs today.
  • Longevity and successorTransient or enduring? If transient, name the artefact it hands off to.
  • RF's neutral seatWhy can RF steward this edge without any actor defecting? If RF would own it, stop.

Where the form is a commons, the older tests still apply on top: open and interoperable (FOSS / PDGI), ownable at the edges, cheap to join and valuable at the margin, trusted by convergence rather than single authority, and tied to an action loop. They are properties of the commons form, not the definition of a border artefact.

4 · Map the actors, then staff the roles

POESI is our actor-mapping tool: sort every actor in a place into one of its five rings, and the artefact you are hunting shows up at the intersection between them.

Place and Owners are the model’s root; Enablers bridge to the deep-domain solution providers; supporting infrastructure (research, policy) is pure-play. Owners can be enablers, not vice versa.

Then, for the artefact you are building, staff the delivery roles. Two of our own maps already name them: the OAQ six roles and the waste canvas's Core / Catalyst / Enabler layers, the same taxonomy. Name a real actor for each; the empty role is where it will stall.

To these six, the multi-year cases add three verbs that recur at every stage: convening the actors, presenting evidence, and lobbying for the rule change nothing else substitutes for.

Measure the gap being crossed, not the aggregation. Not the count of partners or canvases, but: an open commons that exists, an incumbent that joins, a rule that changes, another geography that replicates, an artefact that successfully handed off to its successor.

5 · Three shapes, worked

RF's method has more than one shape. Naming them is half the work.

CaseShapeWin condition
OAQ · airOpen one commons, aggregate fastan incumbent joins (Google opens its sensor data)
Biodiversity · wild + ruralLink stranded registries via devolutiona rights-to-benefit chain finally carries value
Waste · urbanA canvas exposes a chokepoint, then policy plus a marketinnovators get reliable feedstock, freed from tenders

OAQ is the fast, single-commons shape: an open air-quality layer that communities feed and solution actors build on. RF seeded the artefact and aggregated the ecosystem; it built no sensor. Read the OAQ case study →

The biodiversity chain is the slow, multi-registry shape. A People's Biodiversity Register is inert unless it is linked to Community Forest Rights (tenure) and Access and Benefit Sharing (a return), riding a devolution chain from the national authority down to a capable local committee. The artefact is the linkage plus the multi-year coalition, not any single register. It fuses ecology and livelihoods in one place.

Waste is the cross-theme shape. The Socratus and Desta canvas revealed the broken material-recovery facility; a coalition (housing ministry, funders, consultants) turns that into a professionalised-MRF policy and a feedstock-purchasing market that routes innovators around the municipal tenders they never clear. Then it radiates: wet-waste mountains are measured for methane by reusing OAQ, and their leachate is measured against the water-quality theme. One artefact serves three themes; one coalition dissolves several linked dynamics at once.

6 · Start with the data

Before you define an artefact, do the data work. Get the data to answer a real question, and let it surface the hidden dynamics underneath.

Write the data story. A data story is data gathered to answer one honest question about a place or a system, read until the hidden dynamics show themselves: the political economy, the perverse incentives, the broken data integrity that keep the problem stuck. The border artefact is derived from that understanding, never assumed. If no data story exists for your theme, writing one is the first move, and often the most valuable thing RF can seed.

Make this a habit across the team and our partners: pick a question that matters, get the data, and let it draw the linkages between issues. The stories below are examples of the practice, not a menu of artefacts. Read each to understand an issue and see what it connects to; the artefact comes after, and usually spans more than one of them.

the-uncountedwild
India counts elephants and tigers; 8 of 10 conflict species go untallied and snakebite (~50,000 deaths a year) is excluded from conflict policy. Hidden dynamic: what is not counted cannot be governed.
whose-forestwild
Twenty years on, ~3% of Community Forest Rights are recognised; boundaries are mapped but unpublished, so the right is un-auditable. Hidden dynamic: a right you cannot see is a right you cannot claim.
pbr-storywild · rural
2.72 lakh People's Biodiversity Registers, 2 approved, near-zero public access, benefit-sharing at 4%. Hidden dynamic: documentation without devolution is inert.
fpo-storyrural
Farmer Producer Organisations registered on paper but "hollow", formalism masking weak operations. Hidden dynamic: the count of institutions is mistaken for their function.
shg-storyrural
~90 million women in Self Help Groups, in isolation with no shared tracking. Hidden dynamic: scale without interoperability does not compound.
canitlastrural · knowledge
The Panchayat Advancement Index scores "successful" districts draining aquifers 2 to 3x faster than they refill. Hidden dynamic: the wrong metric rewards the wrong outcome.

7 · How to derive the artefact for a theme

The generic recipe. Run it per vertical, per horizontal, and per leaf, one at a time.

This section is the reusable method. The detailed per-theme derivations (which artefact each leaf actually needs, and who sits at its border) are the next phase of work, done one theme at a time.

  1. Start with a question and the data (Section 6). Do the data story first: get the data, surface the hidden dynamics, draw the linkages. The artefact is derived from what you find, not chosen from a list.
  2. Draw the border. Name the few actors on each side and what would cross between them. If it is "everybody", you have not found the edge yet.
  3. Pick the form the edge needs now. Diagnostic, commons, strategy, contract or convening. Expect it to change over the lifecycle.
  4. Set the longevity. Transient or enduring, and if transient, name the successor it should hand off to.
  5. Staff the roles. Fill every row of the role table with a real actor. The empty role is the risk.
  6. Run the lifecycle. Diagnose, convene, re-align, institutionalise, then retire or persist. Present evidence and lobby at every stage.
  7. Watch the intersections. When a theme's artefact touches another theme (waste to air to water), that overlap is a new artefact waiting to be named.
  8. Measure the gap crossed, not the aggregation. Make open, interoperable contribution a condition of RF support.

A border artefact is defined by its border, purpose and longevity, not its material. Candidates are options to test, not commitments; sources are our own data stories (the-uncounted, whose-forest, pbr-story, fpo-story, shg-story, canitlast), the sector hidden-dynamics, the OAQ platform, and the Socratus and Desta waste ecosystem canvas.
